decide records one Decision in a Study: the question it answers, the option chosen, who decided (a human or a proxy, with a declared source if any), the ids of the Study's records it concerns, and the Write sequence its author read. The Study is the only decision authority: a Decision is a record of the Study, kept in a file of its own and never rewritten, and nowhere else. decide calls no model; it records what its caller says, after it has checked the ids and the sequence. deviation records a Declared deviation in the same shape (below).

The command

decide holds the Study's writer lock for the whole command, as every command that writes does. A Decision is made on the Write sequence its author read (chapter 04), and a repeated request is a replay (below). It creates or changes no file outside the Study's folder.

The question and the option are free text, and neither may be empty. The author is human or proxy, recorded by its kind only: a human has no name field. The declared source, for either author, is a text, recorded as given, or as null. A cited id given twice is recorded once and the ids are sorted, so the same ids in another order are the same Decision.

With --json, stdout holds one object, {"ok", "replayed", "decision"}, the same shape as run's with the Decision in place of the Attempt.

The ids a Decision may cite

An id is citable when the Study holds a record with that id, of one of these kinds: an Attempt, an Observation set, an Assessment, a Comparison, a Study artifact (references-<digest>, for example), a revision, a freeze record, an Exposure entry, a Decision, whose id is its decision_id, or a Declared deviation, a withdrawal included. decide looks the id up in the Study's records. A revision is cited by its revision id, the one the Study's records give it (chapter 10); its revision_number is not citable, so that one revision never appears under two ids and "the same Decision" stays one thing. A request id, the Study's id, a path and a file's content digest are not citable either: they name no record. An id the Study does not hold is refused, and nothing is written.

status --json lists every Assessment and every Comparison the Study holds, each as its record stores it (chapter 04), so a caller finds their ids there and, for a Comparison, the two Assessments it depends on. It lists no Observation set: a caller finds those ids in the Attempts' records.

The request identity

A Study has one request_id namespace, for its Attempts, its Decisions and its Declared deviations (chapter 04). "The same Decision" is the same question, option, author, source and cited ids, the ids de-duplicated and sorted; the read sequence is not part of it. The same request_id with the same Decision is a replay: it is found before the stale-sequence check, so a client whose answer was lost can repeat its decide after its own write moved the sequence on. It returns the recorded Decision with replayed true and writes nothing.

The same request_id with another Decision is a conflict (the table of refusals below). It lists the fields that differ in the order of the sentence above, under the Decision record's own field names.

The checks come in the order of every writer: the arguments (the read sequence value first), the Study, the lock, the request identity, the comparison of the read sequence, then the cited ids. A refusal appends nothing to the write journal.

The Decision record

The record is canonical JSON, of the record kind decision of the contract schema (DecisionRecord): validated before it is written and after it is read, with a fault refused (the table of refusals below). Its revision is read under the lock, after the stale-sequence check, so a Decision made after a new revision holds the new one. For a recorded Decision, read_sequence is always write_sequence minus 1.

When a Decision still applies

A Decision no longer applies when any condition of the table below holds. The rule is computed when status reads the Study, from the ids the Decision cites and the records of the Study. It is never stored: nothing is written when a Decision stops applying, the Decision's own file is byte for byte the same, and status takes no lock, advances no Write sequence and writes nothing. No record marks a Decision outdated, and the rule is no general invalidation engine: beside the ids a Decision cites, it follows one hop, from a cited Comparison to its two Assessments, and goes no further.

A cited record made under an earlier revision does not by itself make a Decision outdated: an Assessment may apply its revision's Protocol also to outputs recorded under an earlier revision, and the id a revision reason gives is the revision id a Decision cites. A rescore under a new Scorer version supersedes the earlier Assessment, since the Scorer counts at any version; an Assessment of the same Observation set by another Scorer does not. When several later Assessments exist, the reason names the latest.

"Later" is the one order of the later Assessment, stated once in chapter 10 and read from the write journal (chapter 04). A later Decision on the same question changes nothing: it does not make an earlier one outdated.

The two Assessments of the hop are the Control and the Candidate the Comparison's record names; a record that names no roles has its left Assessment as the Control and its right one as the Candidate. The hop starts from a cited Comparison only: a cited Attempt or Observation set reaches no Assessment. The reasons of the cited ids come first, in the order of the table; then those of the hop, walking the cited Comparisons in the order of the Decision's cited ids, the Control before the Candidate. An Assessment reached through two cited Comparisons gives one reason, at the first. A Decision that cites a Comparison and one of its Assessments can hold two reasons for one later Assessment, one for the cited Assessment and one for the hop. Whether a Decision applies reads the order of the Assessments only, never when the Decision was recorded: a Decision recorded after a later Assessment exists does not apply from its first read.

What happened to the evidence

The evidence of a Decision is the Assessments it cites and the Assessments it has reached, an Assessment being reached when a cited Comparison names it, by the hop above. When some of them have later Assessments, status --json says what happened to the evidence since, in the last key of the history table, and leaves whether the Decision applies as the rule above gives it: a repeat still makes a Decision outdated, and the state is what says that nothing changed.

One line is given per Assessment of the evidence that has a later Assessment of the same Observation set and Scorer id, and that Assessment is the line's anchor. The lines come in the order in which the cited ids first reach their anchors: a cited Assessment at its own id, a cited Comparison's Control before its Candidate. A line names the first cited Comparison that names its anchor, with the anchor's role in it, or none when the anchor is only cited directly. A side that the Study does not hold gives no line. The state of a line is read from the later Assessments of its anchor, and the state of the whole is the strongest of its lines, in the order of the table of states.

A later Assessment counts as rescored or changed only when the earlier Assessment its rescoring names is the anchor or one later than it; otherwise it counts as a repeat. A new Assessment names as earlier the latest one that differs from it (chapter 10), which can be older than the anchor: assessed again on a corrected References set, an Observation set names its Assessment on the set before the correction, and the Items it lists as changed did not change since the anchor. The count of changed Items counts each Item once, however many of its Criteria changed. A line gives the Metric values of the anchor and of the latest later Assessment as their records hold them; no difference of the two is computed, and the Comparison is not updated.

The pending decision

A reviewer who opens a Study finds in status --json what waits for a decision. A Comparison is pending when both its Assessments are of the current revision, its two Assessments have no later Assessment, by the rule of the hop, and no Decision that applies cites it. An Assessment is of the current revision when its record holds a study_revision whose revision is the Study's current one; one whose record holds none, or that the Study does not hold, is not. A Comparison written under a new revision from Assessments of an earlier one is therefore not pending, and neither is a Comparison whose side is not held.

Each pending Comparison comes with one question and three options, the same for every Comparison of every Study, of either Track: the core holds them, no Workload declares them and no client holds them. A client answers with decide, citing the Comparison, with the question's text and the chosen option's text; the Comparison then stops being pending. It does not become pending again when a side gets a later Assessment, since the Comparison then has a later Assessment and the Decision no longer applies; a new compare of the latest Assessments writes another Comparison, which can be pending. Nothing is stored: the list is derived each time status reads the Study, and an answer with an option of its own wording closes the pending decision like any other.

Declared deviations

deviation records one Declared deviation in a Study: one known limit on what results of the Study show, declared in the Study because its records cannot establish it (for example an owner review of References still pending): what is declared, its author, a declared source if any, and the ids of the Study's records it cites. It follows the Decision record's shape: one file of its own, never rewritten, made on the Write sequence its author read and under the writer lock. A derived deviation, such as the order of the Episodes that compare reads from the manifests (chapter 11), is a deviation but not a Declared deviation.

A Declared deviation either declares or withdraws. A declaration cites Attempts and References sets only: an Attempt by its id, the References set of any revision by its Study artifact id. A withdrawal cites exactly one earlier declaration and nothing else, with its own author and text. A declaration covers a side of a Comparison when it cites that side's Attempt, or the References set that side's Assessment was scored against; since compare refuses two Assessments scored against different References sets (chapter 07), a References set covers both sides. A withdrawal covers no side.

When compare writes a Comparison, each declaration in force that covers a side gives it one warning (chapter 07), in the order of the declarations' write_sequence, and the status is computed as without it. A withdrawn declaration gives none. The report shows the warnings each Comparison recorded and reads no Declared deviation.

The checks come in the order of every writer: the arguments, the Study, the lock, the request identity, the comparison of the read sequence, then the ids cited, in this order: an id the Study does not hold; a Declared deviation cited with any other id; several Declared deviations; a withdrawal cited; an id of a kind that cannot be cited; a declaration that is withdrawn already. The first that fails is the refusal, and nothing is written. "The same Declared deviation" of a replay is the same text, author, source and cited ids.

status --json lists every Declared deviation, withdrawals included, in the order they were recorded, with the two keys of the table below that the record does not hold: they are computed when status reads the Study, and never stored. A Decision may cite a Declared deviation, or a withdrawal, which no Declared deviation may.

Limits

Read at view time, never stored

Whether a Decision still applies is computed each time status reads the Study. No record is written when it changes, and the Workloads and the panel do not show an outdated Decision yet.

Free text, no declared question

The question and the option are the caller's words: decide records them as given and checks neither against the question and the options that the core supplies for a pending decision (above). The Study declares no question of its own, so nothing checks that an option is one of a list.

A corrected References set is another set

A corrected References set, added by a new revision (chapter 10), is a Study artifact of its own, with a new artifact_id. A declaration that cites the earlier set does not cover a side whose Assessment was scored against the corrected one; a new declaration that cites the corrected set does.

A Comparison is not updated

A Comparison written before a declaration or a withdrawal is not updated: its file stays as it was, so it holds no warning for a later declaration and keeps the warning of a declaration withdrawn since. A new compare of the same two Assessments is the remedy: it writes another Comparison, with its own id, from the declarations in force then. A new compare of public_check Items appends an Exposure entry, as every Comparison of them does (chapter 12).

Withdrawn stays withdrawn

No Declared deviation may cite a withdrawal, so a declaration once withdrawn stays withdrawn. To declare it again, record a new declaration: it gets a new ID, and covers its sides like any other.

Not Inspect's invalidation

Inspect records a change to a log as an appended update with its author, reason and time, and keeps the values as written; a Declared deviation follows that shape in a record of the Study. It departs on the withdrawal: Inspect's uninvalidate_samples (pinned inspect-ai==0.3.287) clears a sample's invalidation in the log, where a withdrawal is a later record that nothing undoes, beside the declaration it withdraws.

A withdrawal changes no Decision

A Decision that cites a Declared deviation still applies once that Declared deviation is withdrawn: a withdrawn one is still held by the Study, and no reason of the table above names it.

A record without rescoring is a repeat

A later Assessment whose record holds no rescoring key counts as a repeat, as one whose rescoring is null does: what it changed cannot be read from its record.

A null latest value has two causes

The latest value of a Metric in a line is null either because the latest record holds no Metric of that id or because the Metric's value is null in it. The listing of Assessments holds the whole Metric, with its status and its reason.

A Decision is not an Exposure

Recording a Decision reads no public_check Item and writes no freeze record or journal entry of the Exposure journal. It cites ids; it does not show what they hold.
