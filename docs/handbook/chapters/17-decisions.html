decide records one Decision in a Study: the question it answers, the option chosen, who decided (a human or a proxy, with a declared source if any), the ids of the Study's records it concerns, and the Write sequence its author read. The Study is the only decision authority: a Decision is a record of the Study, kept in a file of its own and never rewritten, and nowhere else. decide calls no model; it records what its caller says, after it has checked the ids and the sequence.

The command

decide holds the Study's writer lock for the whole command, as every command that writes does. A Decision is made on the Write sequence its author read (chapter 04), and a repeated request is a replay (below). It creates or changes no file outside the Study's folder.

The question and the option are free text, and neither may be empty. The author is human or proxy, recorded by its kind only: a human has no name field. The declared source, for either author, is a text, recorded as given, or as null. A cited id given twice is recorded once and the ids are sorted, so the same ids in another order are the same Decision.

With --json, stdout holds one object, {"ok", "replayed", "decision"}, the same shape as run's with the Decision in place of the Attempt.

The ids a Decision may cite

An id is citable when the Study holds a record with that id, of one of these kinds: an Attempt, an Observation set, an Assessment, a Comparison, a Study artifact (references-<digest>, for example), a revision, a freeze record, an Exposure entry or a Decision, whose id is its decision_id. decide looks the id up in the Study's records. A revision is cited by its revision id, the one the Study's records give it (chapter 10); its revision_number is not citable, so that one revision never appears under two ids and "the same Decision" stays one thing. A request id, the Study's id, a path and a file's content digest are not citable either: they name no record. An id the Study does not hold is refused, and nothing is written.

status --json lists no Observation set, Assessment or Comparison: a caller finds those ids in the Study's records, and the listing of them in status --json waits for a later change.

The request identity

A Study has one request_id namespace, for its Attempts and its Decisions (chapter 04). "The same Decision" is the same question, option, author, source and cited ids, the ids de-duplicated and sorted; the read sequence is not part of it. The same request_id with the same Decision is a replay: it is found before the stale-sequence check, so a client whose answer was lost can repeat its decide after its own write moved the sequence on. It returns the recorded Decision with replayed true and writes nothing.

The same request_id with another Decision is a conflict (the table of refusals below). It lists the fields that differ in the order of the sentence above, under the Decision record's own field names.

The checks come in the order of every writer: the arguments (the read sequence value first), the Study, the lock, the request identity, the comparison of the read sequence, then the cited ids. A refusal appends nothing to the write journal.

The Decision record

The record is canonical JSON, of the record kind decision of the contract schema (DecisionRecord): validated before it is written and after it is read, with a fault refused (the table of refusals below). Its revision is read under the lock, after the stale-sequence check, so a Decision made after a new revision holds the new one. For a recorded Decision, read_sequence is always write_sequence minus 1.

When a Decision still applies

A Decision no longer applies when any condition of the table below holds. The rule is computed when status reads the Study, from the ids the Decision cites and the records of the Study. It is never stored: nothing is written when a Decision stops applying, the Decision's own file is byte for byte the same, and status takes no lock, advances no Write sequence and writes nothing. No record marks a Decision outdated, and the rule is no general invalidation engine: it reads these three things and nothing else.

A cited record made under an earlier revision does not by itself make a Decision outdated: an Assessment may apply its revision's Protocol also to outputs recorded under an earlier revision, and the id a revision reason gives is the revision id a Decision cites. A rescore under a new Scorer version supersedes the earlier Assessment, since the Scorer counts at any version; an Assessment of the same Observation set by another Scorer does not. When several later Assessments exist, the reason names the latest.

"Later" is the position in the write journal (chapter 04): an Assessment's position is that of the first end entry that names it among the records it wrote. A record that no end entry names counts as position 0, before every journaled record: so does an Assessment written before the write journal existed. Ties are broken by (recorded_at, id), the order rescoring.describe uses. A later Decision on the same question changes nothing: it does not make an earlier one outdated.

Limits

Read at view time, never stored

Whether a Decision still applies is computed each time status reads the Study. No record is written when it changes, and the Workloads and the panel do not show an outdated Decision yet.

Free text, no declared question

The question and the option are the caller's words. The Study declares no question with options yet, so nothing checks that an option is one of a list.

A Decision is not an Exposure

Recording a Decision reads no public_check Item and writes no freeze record or journal entry of the Exposure journal. It cites ids; it does not show what they hold.
