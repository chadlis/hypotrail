With 8 dev Cases, a Comparison of two agent Configurations on Chinook could not tell them apart. The Chinook Workload now holds 100 dev Cases, and one live Campaign ran plain and verify on them, under the conditions of D-131 on chadlis/hypotrail#75 and D-145 on chadlis/hypotrail#75. This chapter says what the Workload holds, how its new References were written, the plan the Campaign followed, the ceiling it ran under, what it recorded, and what its result does not show. Every figure below is read from a file kept in the repository. Chapter 16 keeps its account of the earlier Campaign, on public_check.

What the Workload holds

The dev Partition holds the 8 Cases of the kit, D01 to D08, byte for byte, and 92 Cases written in English after them, D09 to D100. The public_check Partition keeps its 12 Cases. Each Case has one Reference, and the table counts them by the status of that Reference: a table to give, a question to send back for clarification, or a question the database cannot answer. The kit's Cases keep the language of the kit.

How the new References were written

The 92 new References were written before any live call, by two writers who did not see each other's work. A Case author, who wrote no Reference, wrote a pool of candidate Cases in one fixed order, committed before the writers started. Each writer worked alone in a folder outside every checkout that held a copy of the locked database, the questions and a brief, and nothing else: no Reference, no agent answer. For each candidate, each writer gave a status and, for a table, a SQL text. Both SQL texts were run on the locked database and their tables compared by the Scorer's rules; a third reader was kept for a disagreement, and an ambiguous Case was withdrawn. The SQL of the first writer became the Reference's oracle_sql, and the second writer's derivation is its independent check. No agent answer was ever read to settle a Reference.

The review record, examples/chinook/references/chinook-references-2.review.jsonl, lists every candidate in pool order with both writers' answers, whether their tables were equal, and the outcome, after a header that holds the two writers' isolation witnesses. A fresh reader then read the whole set against the Case texts before the first paid call (comment 7187057611 on chadlis/hypotrail#232). Its first reading found a tolerance of 0.05 where 16 two-decimal questions need 0.005, and two Cases whose order the question did not fix; the tolerances were corrected and the two Cases withdrawn, on Cases no agent had answered, and its second reading found nothing more. The new records carry chinook-references/2, the set version in which each was written (chapter 03); no record of the kit's set changed.

The plan

The Campaign followed a plan fixed before any call, traces/campaign-chinook-dev/plan.json. The table prints each of its lines, with when it runs. A value between angle brackets is read from an earlier line's output, as the plan's placeholders say. Only the lines run-plain and run-verify, and the two lines that would invoke each again, call a model; the drive ran them, each once, through its capture script, and kept what each printed in traces/campaign-chinook-dev/commands/. No line needed a rerun. The Decision is a rule fixed in the plan: keep the Configuration with the higher contract_success on dev, and plain on a tie; with a null delta, choose neither yet.

The ceiling and the Repeats

Before the first paid invocation, traces/campaign-chinook-dev/ceiling.json fixed the cost limit of one Episode and bounded what one Episode can cost under it, at the prices of traces/campaign-chinook-dev/price-list.json. run agent stops an Episode with the stop reason cost_limit once its cost reaches the limit, so the bound covers the one call that can overshoot it. The ceiling of an invocation is its number of Episodes times that bound. Three Repeats would have needed a three-Repeat ceiling of at most USD 40 at the smallest round cost limit above the floor the earlier kept logs set; it was higher, so the Campaign ran one Repeat, at the largest round cost limit whose ceiling stays under the USD 80 budget.

What the result does not show

The Comparison carries no interval and no significance claim: one Repeat per Case gives one Episode per Case and side, and the Comparison was written before the interval existed, so it holds none and is never rewritten; the demonstration computes one on a copy after a new revision (chapter 11). The two sides ran one after the other, plain first, not alternated, so the delta may include a drift between the two blocks; compare records the warning COMPARISON_ORDER_NOT_ALTERNATED and the Comparison stays compatible. The spend is an estimate: the logs' cost at the plan's prices, which can differ from what is billed. The shares describe these 100 Cases, on one model, not agents in general, and no public_check Item was run. The proxy Decision records what the plan's rule gave, not that the rule is right.
